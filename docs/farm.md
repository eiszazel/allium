# The farm ambition

Buying an onion farm is the goal. It is not token backing, a community acquisition, or a completed transaction.

The intended owner is a **future founder-owned LLC**, funded with the founder's **post-tax cash**. No LLC formation, land purchase, acreage, partner, closing, or growing season is represented as secured. A seasonal lease or grower partnership could provide a first-season route while the purchase goal remains; neither is an agreement already in place.

## Who owns the field?

The future LLC would own or operate its actual farm arrangement. $ONION holders would not acquire its land, equity, management powers, crops, rent, sale proceeds, or operating income. A token contract does not transfer a deed or control an off-chain company. The founder's responsibilities and any actual commercial agreements are not holder rights.

Farm revenue belongs to the farm's LLC. Data-service revenue belongs to its operator. Neither becomes holder yield, and no farm-income distribution or rent-funded buyback is promised.

## How would it be paid for?

The purchase goal is the founder's responsibility, not a crowdfunding target. Actual creator fees or sales of the founder's own token holdings could be among the founder's sources **only if they occur and are disclosed**. No fee rate, token sale, income, budget, or funding commitment is claimed today.

The account should make actual financing sources, relevant founder wallets and holdings, compensation, fees, sales, and conflicts legible. It should not disguise founder wallets or claim an artificial economic separation between token activity and the founder's money. A disclosed connection still does not grant holders ownership or returns. Market capitalization and trading volume are not money in a farm account.

## Real milestones, real records

If an arrangement is completed, the public story could include permitted, redacted closing or lease records and a farm log covering planting, costs, irrigation and other setbacks, harvests, and crop disposition. Losses, failed arrangements, and abandoned plans belong in that account too.

County, variety, and soil details may be shared where safe and available. No farm photographs, acreage, maps, partners, or ownership evidence are fabricated. Permission and privacy review come before publication: signatures, banking details, wire instructions, and precise GPS or live location data do not belong in the public record.

The [Field Notes](field-notes.md) describe the planned weekly posts and monthly record hashes. A hash can check published bytes, not prove title or authenticate a closing. The farm's own prices and revenue logs would remain separate from the independently sourced [benchmark](benchmark.md); the project farm must not set its benchmark price.

There is no promised acquisition date, guaranteed season, or guaranteed completion. Follow the story—not a claim on the field.
